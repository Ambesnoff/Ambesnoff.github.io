Undergraduate Research Volunteer | January 2024–August 2025

## Design Challenge

Earlier tensioning arrangements left the belts too loose, and the revised assembly could not be tightened adequately. I took the initiative to develop an alternative that fit into the existing rig.

## My Solution

I designed a mechanism in SolidWorks using a T-bar, two parallel rigid brackets, and adjustment bolts. Tightening the bolts pulled one side of the belt mechanism toward the brackets and away from the opposite side, allowing belt tension to be adjusted.

I created fabrication drawings and integrated the design into the larger assembly. The mechanism was subsequently fabricated and installed on the rig.

## Fabrication & Assembly

Working with another undergraduate volunteer, I assembled the rig’s frame, linear rails, belts, and bearings. Our fabrication and assembly work included laser cutting, waterjet cutting, and construction using aluminum components and T-slot framing.

The build also required disassembly and reassembly as the setup developed, giving me practical experience with component fit, belt tension, bearing installation, and assembly sequence.

## Outcome & Learning

My contribution resulted in a custom tensioning mechanism that was fabricated and installed, alongside the mechanical assembly work I shared with another volunteer.

The project strengthened my understanding of how belt tension, bearing loads, bearing crush, structural stiffness, and assembly constraints influence mechanical design. It also gave me experience taking an independently developed idea from CAD and fabrication drawings to installed hardware.
